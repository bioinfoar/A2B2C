About the meeting

The XV Congress of the Argentine Association for Bioinformatics and Computational Biology (A2B2C) provides a framework to foster the exchange of experiences and initiatives among students, professors, researchers, technicians, and professionals involved in activities related to Bioinformatics and Computational Biology.

Date: November 11–12, 2026

Location: IFIBYNE Auditorium, Ciudad Universitaria, Buenos Aires, Argentina

The plenary lectures and presentations will cover various aspects that bring together the branches of bioinformatics and computational biology with the following thematic tracks:

  • Proteomics and Protein Structure and Function
  • Evolutionary, Comparative and Population Genomics
  • Genomics, Transcriptomics, Epigenomics, Metagenomics
  • Biological Databases and Bioimaging
  • Artificial Intelligence and Systems Biology
  • Education
  • IDPFun2 Special Track

Language: All abstracts and posters must be submitted in English. All oral presentations and plenary talks will be conducted in English.

Confirmed Speakers

Rodrigo F. Díaz
ITBA / CONICET / UNSAM, Argentina
Round Table 1 panelist

Rodrigo F. Díaz is Associate Dean of the School of Management and Technology at ITBA, Independent Researcher at CONICET, and professor at UNSAM. His research applies statistical and machine learning methods to detect exoplanets in radial velocity data, separating planetary signals from stellar activity. He founded the Bachelor’s in Data Science at UNSAM and led the creation of ITBA’s Master’s in Artificial Intelligence.

Jimena Rinaldi
CEBEM / UNSAM, Argentina
Round Table 2 panelist

Jimena Rinaldi is a structural biochemist and group leader of the Photobiology and Optogenetics Laboratory at the Institute of Nanosystems, Universidad Nacional de San Martín (UNSAM), Argentina. She earned her degree in Biological Sciences and her PhD in Biological Chemistry at the University of Buenos Aires, studying the structure of the PKA regulatory subunit. As a postdoctoral fellow and later CONICET researcher at the Instituto Leloir, she focused on how bacteria sense light, uncovering the molecular mechanisms of the Brucella blue-light histidine kinase and of bacteriophytochromes, red/far-red photoreceptors. Her work combines X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, biochemistry, and spectroscopy. Her lab now also develops optogenetic tools based on bacteriophytochromes to control proteins of biotechnological and clinical interest. She has led national research grants, supervises doctoral and undergraduate students.

Marcelo A. Martí
UBA / CONICET, Argentina
Round Table 2 panelist

Marcelo A. Martí holds a PhD from the University of Buenos Aires (UBA), is a Senior Researcher at CONICET and an Associate Professor in the Department of Biological Chemistry at the Faculty of Exact and Natural Sciences, UBA. He also serves as Secretary of Graduate Studies at the faculty. His work focuses on bioinformatics, with applications in protein biochemistry and clinical genomics. He combines molecular simulations and genomic data analysis to study protein function, contribute to drug design, and improve the interpretation of variants associated with rare diseases.

He has authored more than 200 scientific publications and led numerous national and international research projects, with a strong commitment to training researchers. He contributed to the creation of bioinformatics courses and the UBA School of Clinical Genomics. His distinctions include the 2018 Houssay Award and recognition in MIT Technology Review’s TR35 Argentina-Uruguay in 2012. He is a scientific co-founder of Bitgenia, an Argentine genomics and bioinformatics company focused on diagnostic and precision medicine applications.

Esteban Mocskos
UBA / CONICET, Argentina
Round Table 2 panelist

Esteban Mocskos is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at the Faculty of Exact and Natural Sciences, University of Buenos Aires, and an Independent Researcher at CONICET. He directs the Interdisciplinary High-Performance Computing Laboratory (LICAR), whose research spans computer architecture, distributed systems, and large-scale applications.

He participates in the management and operation of HPC infrastructure at the Center for Computational Simulation for Technological Applications (CSC-CONICET), which hosted the TUPAC supercomputer for several years. He currently coordinates the governance committee of Clementina XXI, the high-performance computing system that marked Argentina’s first entry in the TOP500 ranking.

His experience combines the acquisition, administration, and management of high-performance computing systems, the coordination of technical teams, and research in computer architecture and distributed systems.

He has also contributed to the regional and international HPC community as a co-founder and Steering Committee member of CARLA (the Latin American and Caribbean High-Performance Computing Conference) and as General Co-Chair of ISCA 2024, the leading international computer architecture conference, held in Latin America for the first time in its 50 editions.

Rodrigo Nores
IDACOR / CONICET / Universidad Nacional de Córdoba, Argentina

Rodrigo Nores is a bioanthropologist specializing in paleogenomics. He received his PhD in Chemical Sciences from the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba (UNC) in 2006. He currently works as a CONICET Researcher at the Instituto de Antropología de Córdoba (IDACOR) and is a Full Professor in the Anthropology undergraduate program at the Faculty of Philosophy and Humanities (UNC). His work focuses on reconstructing the evolutionary history of human populations and pathogens in South America through ancient DNA analysis, conducting research in collaboration with international reference centers such as the Department of Genetics at Harvard University and the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology (MPI-EVA).

Gábor Erdös
ELTE, Hungary

Assistant Professor and Principal Investigator at the Department of Biochemistry at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Hungary, where he leads the Protein Dynamics Research Group. Trained as a bioengineer, he holds a PhD in structural biochemistry and has a decade of experience in bioinformatics. His career spans ten years of academic research alongside specialized experience in the pharmaceutical industry, with professional activity in Argentina and extensive collaborations across Europe, particularly in Italy and Israel. His research expertise centers on the characterization of intrinsically disordered proteins through the integration of biophysical concepts and advanced computational approaches. His work focuses on developing novel predictive methods for both general and functional protein disorder, with the goal of bridging artificial intelligence and biophysics. To this end, he employs a broad range of methodologies that includes machine learning, molecular dynamics simulations, and statistical mechanics to investigate the complex behavior and functional roles of intrinsically disordered proteins. He serves as Co-lead of the ELIXIR IDP Community and as the Hungarian Representative for COST. He is also a core member of the ML4NGP COST action, IDP2Biomed twinning project and a contributor of the IDPFun2 project.

Salvador Capella-Gutierrez
BSC / INB–ELIXIR Spain

Salvador Capella-Gutierrez, PhD, leads the Technologies for Biomedical Research Laboratory at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC), as well as the Coordination team of the Spanish National Bioinformatics Institute (INB). INB is the Spanish node of ELIXIR, the European research infrastructure for life science data. Salvador joined the BSC in July 2017 from the Spanish National Cancer Research Center (CNIO) in Madrid. A bioinformatician with a solid technological background in both Computer Engineering and Health Sciences, Salvador received his PhD in Bioinformatics in 2012, awarded by the Center for Genomic Regulation and Pompeu Fabra University in Barcelona, Spain.

His current focus is on developing long-term infrastructure to facilitate the scientific benchmarking and technical evaluation of research software in Life Sciences, including tools, web services, and workflows. More recently, he is interested in how to further elevate those efforts to continue serving the research community in the fast-evolving AI context.

Ana Tereza de Vasconcelos

Jiri Vondrasek

Head of the Bioinformatics Group at the Institute of Organic Chemistry and Biochemistry of the Czech Academy of Sciences (IOCB Prague) and Head of ELIXIR CZ. His research focuses on structural bioinformatics, computational structural biology, molecular modeling, protein evolution, and biomolecular interactions.

Ariela Vergara
Universidad de Talca, Chile

Researcher at the Center for Bioinformatics, Simulation and Modeling at Universidad de Talca. Her research focuses on computational structural biology of membrane proteins, molecular modeling, docking simulations, and molecular dynamics.

ELIXIR-GOBLET TtT Workshop

Schedule: November 13–14, 9:00–17:00. Venue: Cero+Infinito.

This course introduces trainers in the life sciences to learning principles, training techniques, lesson, session, course, and material design as well as assessment and feedback. It offers guidance, ideas and tips for designing, developing and delivering training/teaching activities, all based on research-driven educational principles. Examples and discussions will also focus on the challenges presented by academic teaching. This course has been developed for and by trainers in bioinformatics but is suitable for all trainers and educators in higher education. It is part of the ELIXIR-GOBLET TtT programme.

IDPfun2 Workshop 1: Computational Tools for the Prediction of Intrinsic Disorder in Proteins

This session will introduce the concept of Intrinsically Disordered Proteins (IDPs) and explore how modern bioinformatics and AI-based tools can identify and characterize these regions directly from protein sequences. Through accessible examples and practical applications, participants will learn how computational predictions can help us better understand protein function, regulation, and their roles in health and disease.

IDPfun2 Workshop 2: Molecular Network Resources of the European Bioinformatics Institute

This workshop will introduce key systems biology resources developed by the Molecular Networks Team at EMBL-European Bioinformatics Institute, in particular the IntAct database of molecular interactions, the Complex Portal, and the Reactome database of biomolecular pathways. Language Models Applied to Biological Sequence Analysis

This workshop offers a theoretical and hands-on introduction to the use of language models for biological sequence analysis. We will explore how these models process protein and DNA sequences, identify context-dependent relationships, and generate numerical representations that can be applied to different bioinformatics problems. Through examples and guided exercises, participants will work with sequences, analyze the representations obtained, and interpret their potential applications. Current uses in genomics and proteomics will also be discussed, along with the criteria needed to evaluate results, recognize model limitations, and use these models critically in research contexts.

Schedule: November 13, 9:00–17:00. Venue: Cero+Infinito, Ciudad Universitaria.

Trainers: Clara Campos and Gabriel Iglesias. Places: 40.
